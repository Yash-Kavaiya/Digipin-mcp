How DIGIPIN works

India Post's DIGIPIN divides the country's bounding box (2.5°–38.5° N, 63.5°–99.5° E) into a 4 × 4 grid. Each of the 16 cells gets one of 16 symbols. Repeat inside the chosen cell — 10 times — and you get a code like 39J-49L-L8T4 that points at a cell of roughly 3.8 m × 3.8 m.

  • Globe — click anywhere. The nested outlines are the level 1 → 4 cells of your DIGIPIN.
  • India 3D — click a cell to drill down one level at a time; use the breadcrumb to zoom back out.
  • 🧊 All 10 levels (button under the India 3D map, or key F) — your code as 10 stacked map plates, each with place names and real streets when zoomed in, showing how each character narrows the area 4×.

PIN codes come from OpenStreetMap postcodes and the India Post office directory (api.postalpincode.in), looked up live from your browser. If those are unreachable, the nearest built-in city hub is shown and marked approximate. DIGIPIN itself is computed locally and works offline.
